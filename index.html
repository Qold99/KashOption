When comparing spark plugs online or in stores, check the exact part number, packaging, seller information and vehicle application. A low price or product photo alone cannot establish authenticity.

Common Buying Risks

A spark plug that does not match the engine specification may contribute to poor running or misfires. Confirm the correct part before installation.

Check the manufacturer's specified plug type, heat range, reach and resistor design for your engine.

Inspect packaging and product condition, and buy from a supplier who can explain the product reference and source.

Iridium vs Standard Copper Plugs: Lifespan, Voltage Load, and Fuel Efficiency

Guide 03 • Combustion Physics

Why the Correct Spark Plug Heat Range Matters

Heat range describes how quickly a spark plug transfers heat from its firing end to the cylinder head. The correct range is engine-specific. Using the wrong range can contribute to fouling or overheating, so verify the manufacturer's recommendation.

Guide 04 • Fitment Precision

The Chassis Number Protocol: Why VIN Matching Prevents Fitment Disasters

Vehicles with the same model name can use different engines and spark plugs across model years and markets. A chassis or VIN, engine code and production year help narrow down the correct application, but the final part number should still be confirmed against a reliable catalogue.
