Guide 02 • Metallurgy & Performance

Iridium vs Standard Copper Plugs: Lifespan, Voltage Load, and Fuel Efficiency

While copper plugs have a melting point of 1,085°C, Iridium withstands up to 2,466°C. In modern high-compression Toyota and Honda engines, installing standard copper plugs increases the electrical burden on your ignition coils, leading to premature coil blowout and higher fuel consumption.

Ask Our Technical Desk
Guide 03 • Combustion Physics

Why Incorrect Heat Ranges Destroy Coils and Cause Engine Knock

A spark plug must maintain a self-cleaning tip temperature between 450°C and 870°C. Installing a plug that is too hot causes pre-ignition detonation (engine pinging), while a plug that is too cold causes carbon fouling, cylinder misfires, and catalytic converter clogging.

Ask Our Technical Desk
Guide 04 • Fitment Precision

The Chassis Number Protocol: Why VIN Matching Prevents Fitment Disasters

Two identical-looking Toyota Corollas or Honda Civics can require completely different spark plug thread reaches (19mm vs 26.5mm) depending on whether they were assembled for the North American, European, or Japanese domestic market. Chassis VIN matching eliminates this hazard completely.
